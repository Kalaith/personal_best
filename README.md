# Personal Best

You're a personal trainer running a gym out of a backyard shed. Two clients
walk in the door wanting to run the same marathon twelve weeks from now — and
they could hardly be more different.

- **Jordan Reyes**, 20, a student and footballer. Fit, competitive, and
  chasing a sub-3:30 finish.
- **Margaret "Peg" Holloway**, 60, a retired librarian who has never run
  before. Anxious, determined, and running for her late husband. She just
  wants to cross the line inside the six-hour cut-off.

Same race, same twelve weeks. Your job is to get both of them there — and
to learn that what works for one can break the other.

## How a season plays

1. **Meet your clients.** Each arrives with a goal, an intake assessment and
   a personality that shapes how they like to be coached.
2. **Build a routine.** Up to four exercises, each with sets and a load —
   easy runs, long runs, tempo runs, intervals, squats, lunges, step-ups and
   more. An advisor suggests a starting plan and warns you when something
   doesn't suit the client.
3. **Plan the week.** From the weekly planner you book sessions, review
   routines, shop for equipment and see how everyone is holding up.
4. **Coach the session.** This is the heart of the game: you're in the gym
   with your client, reading how they're coping and deciding when to push
   and when to ease off.
5. **Get paid and review.** After each session you see the progress they
   made, what worked, and what to try next time.
6. **Hit the milestones.** Each four-week training block ends with a test —
   a 30-minute run, a half marathon, a 30 km dress rehearsal.
7. **Race day.** After a taper week, your clients line up for the Harbour
   City Marathon. You find out how they did, and why.

## Coaching a session

Every exercise is played on a sliding bar: a marker sweeps back and forth and
you press to stop it in the target zone. Hit the centre for a Perfect. How
well you coach scales how much your client gains from the whole session.

The bar takes on a different meaning in each coaching moment:

- **Check-in** — ask the right question as they walk in. Slept badly? Sore
  legs? Stressed? Getting it right sets up the rest of the session.
- **Pace coaching** — pick the effort they can actually hold today.
- **Technique** — spot the form fault the moment it appears, then choose the
  right cue.
- **Encouragement** — calm, upbeat, competitive or informative? It depends
  on who they are *and* what the moment needs. An exhausted client needs
  something different from one who's flying.
- **Effort management** — continue, reduce, or stop.

### Push or hold back

At peak moments the bar splits in two: **Push** or **Hold back**. Watch your
client's face, breathing and form:

- **Green** — fresh and smiling. Push them.
- **Amber** — working hard. Push in the build and peak blocks; hold back
  during base training and the taper.
- **Red** — form breaking down, grimacing, slowing. Hold back. Push now and
  you risk fatigue or an injury that costs them a week.

Pushing harder isn't always better. Knowing when to stop is a win.

## Between sessions

Your clients keep training on their own between appointments, so the routine
you set matters even when you're not there. Keep an eye on:

- **Fatigue** — too much and they arrive tired and race flat. Rest days and
  the taper week let them recover.
- **Motivation** — skipped weeks and bad sessions wear it down.
- **Rapport** — good coaching builds trust, and trust makes coaching easier.
- **Life** — Jordan has exams, Peg has her grandchildren, and sometimes it
  rains.

From week 8 the planner shows a race-day projection, so you know whether each
goal is on track, in reach, or slipping away.

## Growing the gym

Session fees and goal bonuses pay for new kit, and every purchase changes how
you can train:

- **Foam rollers** for recovery work
- **Heart-rate monitor** to see your client's exact energy
- **Rowing machine** for low-impact endurance
- **Treadmill** so rain doesn't ruin a run
- **Barbell and rack** and **track membership** once your reputation grows
- **Shed extension** for more equipment space

Passing milestones and reaching marathon goals builds your reputation and
opens up bigger upgrades.

## What it teaches

Personal Best is also about understanding *why* training works: why long
runs build endurance, why intervals build pace, why strength work keeps form
together late in a race, and why rest is part of the plan. Every session
review and race result explains what made the difference.

The training model is a simplified game system, not medical or training
advice.
